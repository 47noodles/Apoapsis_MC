# 17 — Worlds, biomes and life

> Design by Opus, from 47noodles' direction (chat, 11 Oct 2026): *"Each world should use its own unique world gen, perhaps stemming from vanilla… at least 50 unique biomes, unique animals"*, then *"start on vanilla world but have each planet follow that same idea. It could be forked to include those NMS style random spawn stuff."* Research inputs: R9 (26.3 worldgen pipeline), R7 (LOD and orbit rendering). Licences marked ✔ were checked on Modrinth on 2026-10-11.

## 17.0 Rules

1. **You start on vanilla worldgen.** Verdance is generated by vanilla's own overworld settings, with every vanilla biome, mob and structure, wrapped onto the cube-sphere (`03` §3.2) with latitude driving temperature (`07` §7.2).
2. **Every other world gets its own generator, derived from vanilla's system:** its own noise router, biome source, material rules, features and structures.
3. **At least 50 unique biomes on every major world.**
   - *Major worlds:* Verdance, Pale, Cinder, Ochre, Thalassa, Ember, Glacis, Murk, Hollow.
   - *Smaller sets, with reasons:* Tempest has no surface, so it gets about 12 altitude and storm "biomes". Rook, Pawn and the Wanderer are rubble bodies. The Shoals are asteroid classes.
4. **Every world has its own life,** following its chemistry (§17.4). On most worlds that means plants and animals; airless Pale has machine life instead, and the rubble bodies have none.
5. **Every world looks different without shaders:** its own block palette, colour maps, fog, sky, light tint and particles (§17.3).
6. **Hand-designed, not random.** Every server's Ochre has the same species and biomes, so wikis and shared knowledge work. NMS-style random generation is a fork option (§17.6).

## 17.1 Vanilla as the foundation

Vanilla's worldgen is data-driven (R9 §1):
- a **noise router** of density functions
- a **multi-noise biome source** over temperature, humidity, continentalness, erosion, weirdness and depth
- **material rules** (renamed from surface rules in 26.3)
- placed features and structures

The cheapest robust path, which Ad Astra and Galacticraft also use (R9 finding 4), is the **vanilla `minecraft:noise` generator with per-world settings**. Custom Java density functions handle the cube-sphere mapping, impact craters, lava seas, ice shells and Thalassa's abyss. Each world is a datapack plus a few Java density functions, so pack makers can edit any world without touching code.

## 17.2 The biome composer: 50+ biomes per world without 400 hand-made ones

Vanilla reaches about 64 overworld biomes through *families with variants* (forest → birch, dark, flower, old-growth). Apoapsis does the same deliberately, with a tool.

**Per world, designers author:**
- a **block palette**: the world's own stones, soils, sands, ices and crystals (original art, §17.3);
- what each **climate axis** means there. On Ochre, "temperature" is latitude plus elevation and "humidity" is ancient ice content. On Thalassa, "continentalness" is depth;
- **8–12 biome families**, growing from the 4–7 signature biomes listed in `07`.

**The composer crosses** family × climate band × elevation band × weirdness modifier. Each variant gets its own terrain-shaping weights, surface palette, flora set, fauna spawn table, sky, fog and water colours, particles, ambient sound and hazards.

**Distinctness rule (no padding):** a variant counts as a unique biome only if it differs from its siblings in **at least three** of terrain shape, surface palette, flora, fauna, sky/fog colour and hazard.

**A CI coverage report per world** checks biome count (≥ 50), area share (none below 0.3%), a distinctness score, and that every biome reads differently on the orbital map (`07` §7.0 rule 4).

**Worked example: Ochre's families and some of their variants**

| Family | Example variants |
|---|---|
| Rust Dunes | Barchan fields · star dunes · frost dunes (polar) · black-basalt dunes · dune-locked craters |
| Canyon Labyrinth | Narrow slot canyons · terraced mesas · landslide fans · canyon-floor ice lakes · wind-carved arches |
| Tharsis Flank | Lava plains · collapsed lava tubes · summit caldera · ash fields · fissure vents |
| Polar Layered Deposits | Spiral troughs · CO₂ geyser fields · layered ice cliffs · seasonal frost plains |
| Chaos Terrain | Jumbled blocks · sinkhole fields · collapsed ice pockets |
| Perchlorate Flats | Salt crusts · evaporite pans · toxic dust plains |
| Crater Fields | Fresh rayed craters · ancient eroded basins · crater-lake ice |

That's ten families giving 36 named variants already. Elevation and latitude bands push it past 50 without padding.

## 17.3 A visual identity per world

- **Colour signature:** grass, foliage and water colour maps per biome (vanilla's colour map system), fog colour and density, sky and cloud colour, cloud height, star brightness, and sun size and colour (light tint). These are driven through 26.x environment attributes (R9 §2) and our sky renderer (`03` §3.6).
- **Own block palettes**, all original textures from the AI art pipeline with art-lead review (`16` §16.2). For example:
  - Ochre: rustrock, redslate, perchlorite.
  - Glacis: glacite, brine ice, lineae salt.
  - Murk: tholin sand, methane ice.
  - Cinder: slag glass, terminator basalt.
- **Particles and sound:** dust, spores, ash, snow, sulphur fumes, ice crackle and methane rain, with each world's ambient bed (`16` §16.3).
- **Optional shader hints:** with Iris installed, Apoapsis exposes per-world uniforms (planet tint, haze density, ring shadow) for shader packs to use. Without shaders, the look comes from palettes and environment attributes, which is the baseline every world must hit.

## 17.4 Life: unique plants and animals on every world

**Each world's life follows its chemistry.** Even the harsh worlds get life, just strange life:

| World | Life (examples) |
|---|---|
| Verdance | All vanilla mobs, plus gliders, tide crabs, glass moths, cliff nesters, Aurora Taiga elk |
| Pale | No organic life. Precursor **regolith mites**, machine "life" that grazes on metal |
| Cinder | Terminator-belt extremophiles: crystal grazers, heat-vent tube colonies, glass-shell crawlers |
| Ochre | Brine-ice cave dwellers, microbial mats in chaos-terrain ice, dust-skimming spore drifters |
| Thalassa | Lanternfish, kelp eels, ice seals, siphonophore swarms, vent tube worms, **the Leviathan** |
| Ember | Sulphur chemotrophs at the vents, plume-riding spore clouds |
| Glacis | Vent grazers, glass jellies, ice-borers, the subsurface ocean's food web |
| Murk | Cryo-life in the methane seas: slow azotosome swimmers, tholin-crusted shore crawlers (real Titan speculation, turned into gameplay) |
| Tempest | Balloon jellies, storm rays, sky-kelp rafts |
| Hollow | The overgrown Arboretum ecology and Precursor constructs |

**Creature kit** (modular, so dozens of species are affordable):
- **Body plans:** quadruped, hexapod, serpentine, avian, floater, swimmer, crawler, sessile colony.
- **Parts:** heads, limbs, tails, shells, fins, sails, bladders, crystal growths.
- **Behaviours:** grazer, hunter, flocker, burrower, glider, swimmer, symbiont, scavenger, territorial.
- **Adaptations:** the world sets each species' size, speed, gravity response, heat, pressure and radiation tolerance.
- Models are modular Blockbench part sets with shared animation rigs, made in the AI art pipeline and reviewed.

**Flora kit:** trunks and stems, fronds, caps, bulbs, crystals and coral forms, assembled by part grammars (L-system-style growth rules) into trees and plants. Growth reacts to light, heat and pressure tolerance.

**Targets per world:**
- *Living worlds* (Verdance additions, Thalassa, Glacis, Tempest, Hollow): 15–25 fauna and 25–40 flora species.
- *Extremophile worlds:* 6–12 fauna and 10–20 flora species (Pale: 6–12 machine species and no flora).

**Every species has a use and a record:** drops and resources (bio-mass, xenobiology samples, materials, dyes, lift gas), a **Discovery Registry** entry with first-scan naming rights (`14` §14.5), and Survey Points.

## 17.5 Seeing far: Distant Horizons and the planet LOD

- **Distant Horizons** (LGPL-3.0 ✔, 26.3 ✔, NeoForge ✔) ships in the open pack as an optional dependency.
- **Apoapsis registers its own Distant Horizons LOD generator** for every planet dimension. It feeds DH columns computed **straight from the planet's noise**, the same Imprint formula as the orbit view (`03` §3.2.5), instead of building real chunks, so far terrain is cheap and render distance is enormous.
  - **Proof it works on 26.3:** *Distant Horizons SeedGen* (All Rights Reserved ✔, 26.3) computes LODs directly from the seed for noise-based generators through DH's world-generator API. So DH accepts generator plug-ins.
  - **Ours is written from scratch** (clean-room, `09`) for cube-sphere faces and every planet. SeedGen handles noise-based generators (vanilla, Terralith, Tectonic) but can't read our cube-sphere density functions.
- **Multiplayer, with seed privacy:** the client computes far terrain itself from **terrain-shape sub-seeds** (height and climate). The server never sends the ore or structure seeds, so distant terrain renders locally without letting anyone seed-crack for ores or Precursor vaults.
- **Hand-off chain, all from one formula:** real chunks near you → our DH LOD in the middle distance → the Imprint sphere at altitude and from orbit (`03` §3.3.2). Everything comes from the same generator, so there are no seams between the layers.
- **Cube-sphere details:** LOD columns use the same face mapping and halos. Curvature in the DH layer comes through a compat hook if DH's pipeline allows it; otherwise curvature applies to near chunks and the Imprint sphere covers altitude. *To verify in Stage 3.*
- **Explored terrain:** DH stores real chunks you've seen as normal, and our generator only fills in the unexplored.
- **Voxy** is All Rights Reserved ✔ and Fabric-only (26.2 ✔), so it isn't supported in the pack.

## 17.6 Fork note: an NMS-style procedural universe

*Recorded at 47noodles' request as a possible fork or a later official mode, not core scope.*

- **Core Apoapsis:** everyone starts on Verdance in a hand-designed Meridian system.
- **A fork could go full No Man's Sky:**
  - Players **spawn on different procedurally generated worlds in different parts of a galaxy**, find each other through the galaxy map and open comms networks (`15` §15.6), and travel to meet.
  - Every planet's biomes, colours, plants and animals are **generated per seed** from the same composer and kits (§17.2–17.4), with randomised parameters.
- **Why it's cheap to fork later:** the composer, the kits, the planet JSON and The Reach's system generator (`14` §14.4) are already parameterised. A random-spawn mode is mostly a galaxy placer, a species randomiser and a spawn picker on top.
- **Trade-offs:** no shared wiki knowledge, quality control on random species, many more runtime dimensions (R9 §4), and harder balancing.
