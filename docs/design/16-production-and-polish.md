## 16.2 Visual identity *(summary; full design in `13`)*

- **Look:** "NASA-punk": a polished space game first and Minecraft second (`13` §13.0). Readable 16×16 pixel art with a restrained palette per material family: steel greys, brass and zinc warm metals, Mekanism-compatible tier colours. Industrial details (rivets, hazard stripes, panel seams) carry the engineering mood. **Never derived from vanilla pixels** (`09` §9.8).
- **One palette per world.** Each planet has a signature palette and **light tint**: Cinder's amber noon, Ochre's butterscotch haze, Murk's sepia murk, Glacis's ice-blue. It's applied through environment attributes and our sky renderer (`03` §3.6), so worlds look different with no shaders at all.
- **UI design system:** one component kit for the HUD, terminals, map view and navball. It covers type scale, colours, iconography, motion and sound cues. It's documented in Penpot (open source, which suits the charter), and every screen is built from the kit.
- **VFX library:** engine plumes (pressure-scaled), re-entry plasma, dust and methane storms, cryovolcano plumes, auroras, ring shadows, docking lights, weld sparks, decompression bursts. Each effect is budgeted for performance.
- **Ship livery:** per-block paint, ship colour schemes and decals. SE's paint and skins are much-loved, and this one is cheap, so it's in at 1.0.
- **Without shaders:** Apoapsis has to look good with no shader pack. Iris shader support is a bonus (Iris is an optional extra, `09`).
- **AI-created art pipeline** (47noodles' call). Claude authors much of the art directly, as reproducible source rather than one-off images:
  - palette-locked pixel textures generated by scripts, so anyone can re-run, tweak and extend them;
  - Blockbench models as JSON;
  - the UI kit as SVG;
  - shaders and VFX parameters.
  A human art lead reviews everything against the AAA target, and every AI-made asset is labelled in its REUSE metadata (`09` §9.8).

## 16.3 Audio

- **Physics-aware sound:** sound carries through air, is muffled through hulls, and disappears in vacuum. In space you hear only what reaches you through your suit, the hull you're touching, or radio (`15` §15.6).
- **Engine and machine sound:** every engine family has its own layered sound (ignition, throttle, shutdown), and pressure changes the timbre: thunderous at sea level, a thin hiss in vacuum.
- **Planet ambience:** wind, storms, ice creaks, methane rain, vent rumble, each world with its own soundscape.
- **Adaptive music:** an original soundtrack (CC BY 4.0) with stems that react to context: launch, orbit, landing, discovery, danger, the comet.
- **Spoken lines:** the Custodian and key story moments, recorded by community voice actors (CC BY) with subtitles. If voice isn't ready, the text and subtitle system ships first.

## 16.4 Story and writing

- **Story bible:**
  - **Custodian:** an arc from broken AI to unreliable ally.
  - **Precursors:** who they were, why they summoned the *Arkwright*, what the Heart is.
  - **Factions:** the voices of the Free Traders, Salvagers, Remnant and Sentinels.
  - **Timeline:** the history of the *Arkwright* expedition.
- **Delivery:** environmental storytelling first (wrecks, ruins, logs), then the Custodian Log (`04`), then optional voice. No long cutscenes. Short in-engine camera moments, such as a first-orbit "Earthrise" over Verdance, launch countdowns and the Heart's awakening.
- **Endings:** reach the Heart, then choose to **open the gate** (The Reach, `14` §14.4) or **seal it** (stay and build Meridian). The choice is per server, and either way the universe keeps going.
- **Writing team:** one lead writer keeps the voice consistent. All text is CC BY and translatable.

## 16.5 Onboarding, controls and accessibility

- **First hours:** guided Age 0–1 quests, Holo-Manual animated guides, and a **flight school** (a scripted test range on Verdance: hover, land, orbit) that pays Survey Points.
- **Controllers:** **Controlify** (LGPL-3.0 ✔, 26.3 ✔, NeoForge ✔) ships in the open pack for gamepads, with Apoapsis bindings for both flight schemes (`06` §6.1).
- **Joystick and HOTAS:** an Apoapsis input layer maps any axis to throttle, pitch, yaw, roll and translation, with deadzones and curves. Flight-sim players will expect it.
- **Accessibility:** colour-blind HUD palettes, scalable UI, subtitles with speaker tags, hold-to-toggle options, motion-sickness options (camera roll damping, FOV), autopilot assists, and difficulty presets (Δv multiplier, forgiving re-entry, no meltdowns).
- **Localisation:** keys from day one and community translation through an open platform (e.g. Weblate). The UI must fit German and Japanese string lengths.

## 16.6 Front-end experience

- **Title screen:** Verdance from orbit, drawn by our own planet renderer, with Pale rising.
- **World-creation wizard:** universe scale (`compact`, `classic`, `real`), server profile (`15` §15.1), story or sandbox, difficulty preset, seed, and system (Meridian, or later a procedural system).
- **Loading and transitions:** themed screens and the link/transition effects (`14` §14.1).
- **Credits screen:** every CC BY contributor, auto-generated from REUSE metadata. That's attribution required by our own licence, made visible.

## 16.7 Quality and live operations

- **Automated tests:**
  - `orbit` unit tests.
  - NeoForge **GameTest** worlds for grids, seams, sealing, proxies and staging.
  - Headless server soak tests (24 h with bots).
  - A perf benchmark scenario in CI (`03` §3.7).
  - Render smoke tests on OpenGL and Vulkan, with and without Sodium.
- **Save migration:** every version hop (`03` §3.0) ships a tested **world upgrade path**: grid files, orbital state and attachments are versioned, with migration code and a pre-upgrade backup prompt. Losing worlds is the fastest way to lose a community.
- **Crash reports:** an *opt-in*, privacy-first crash reporter (self-hostable, open source, no personal data), plus an "export debug bundle" button for bug reports.
- **Release channels:** nightly, alpha, beta, release candidate and stable. Server operators pin a channel.
- **Patch notes for every version** (47noodles' rule): player notes from the release template, an in-game **What's new** screen (from 0.4), a CHANGELOG entry, and upgrade notes covering saves, servers, API changes and Minecraft hops (`12` §12.6).
- **Balance tooling:** data-driven balance sheets (Δv per Age, resource-flow simulations, engine tables) generated from the same JSON the game loads, so designers tune in data, not code.
- **Security:**
  - The Flight Computer Lua sandbox: instruction budgets, no file or network access, memory caps.
  - Duplication-exploit tests on grid split, merge and connectors.
  - A permission system for admin commands.

## 16.8 Modding and extensibility (the open-source dream)

- **Three tiers of extension:**
  1. **Data:** planets, star systems, engines, blocks' mass, biomes, quests and recipes are all JSON, as datapacks.
  2. **Scripts:** KubeJS bindings (LGPL, optional) for pack makers.
  3. **Java API:** a stable, versioned `apoapsis-api` artifact covering custom grid blocks, thruster types, atmosphere models, orbital events and proxy frames, with semantic versioning and a deprecation policy.
- **SDK:** example addon repos (a custom planet, a new engine, a new proxy frame), a Gradle template, and API Javadoc.
- **Docs site:** player wiki plus developer docs. Block and engine pages are **generated from the game's data**, so the wiki never drifts from the game.
- **Standalone libraries:** physics, planets and sky rendering, orbit, Ages gating, comms networks, the Lua flight computer, Kinetics and the UI kit each ship as their own mod or library, so other projects can depend on just the part they want (`03` §3.0.1).
- **Addons welcome:** an "Apoapsis-compatible" tag guideline for third-party mods. Because the code is MIT, forks and total conversions are allowed and encouraged.

## 16.9 Distribution

- **Players:** a Modrinth `.mrpack` (core plus optional extras, `09` §9.5) that installs in one click in open launchers such as Prism Launcher. An official CurseForge listing for reach.
- **Servers:** a server pack, an official **Docker image** with sane defaults per server profile, and a configuration guide for each profile.
- **Presence:** a website (open source, static), trailer and press kit, with devlogs per stage.
- **Minecraft rules:** we never redistribute Minecraft. We follow Mojang's EULA and usage guidelines on mods and on any monetisation (§16.10).

## 16.10 Community, governance and funding

- **Decision-making:**
  - **Maintainers** own areas (physics, rendering, worlds, gameplay, art, docs).
  - Big design changes go through an **RFC** (a short design doc reviewed in the open) and are recorded as **ADRs** (architecture decision records), so newcomers can see why things are the way they are.
  - These design docs are RFC zero.
- **Contributor path:** CONTRIBUTING.md (clean-room rules, licences, DCO), "good first issue" labels, an art contribution guide, a translation guide, and a code of conduct (Contributor Covenant).
- **Transparency:** public roadmap (`12`), public stage boards, open playtest servers.
- **Funding (optional):** Open Collective or GitHub Sponsors with public spending, used for hosting playtest servers, art commissions and tools. Any monetisation must follow Mojang's EULA and commercial usage guidelines. *Check those before setting anything up.*
- **Recognition:** in-game credits (§16.6), contributor roles, and named megaprojects on official servers.
